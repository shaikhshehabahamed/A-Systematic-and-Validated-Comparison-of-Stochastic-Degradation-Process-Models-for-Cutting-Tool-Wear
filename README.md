# A-Systematic-and-Validated-Comparison-of-Stochastic-Degradation-Process-Models-for-Cutting-Tool-Wear

A cutting tool's remaining life depends on which degradation-process model describes its wear, yet prior studies typically pick one family, most often Gamma, Inverse Gaussian, or Wiener, without testing alternatives or the structural assumptions built into them. This paper fits and compares ten process families, three base families, four structural extensions, and a generalization of Inverse Gaussian, on flank-wear data from nine tools across three milling machines, validated with calibration testing, cross-validation, model recovery, and dependence-robust resampling. No single family wins on every criterion: the lowest raw AIC on every machine belongs to a family that fails calibration everywhere. Among the calibrated families, the generalized variant is most consistently well-supported, with plain Inverse Gaussian a close runner-up. Truncating Wiener's increments to rule out negative wear reaches calibration on two of three machines. A cluster-robust non-nested test leaves most pairwise comparisons insignificant after correction. Random-effects extensions of Gamma and Inverse Gaussian detect no tool-to-tool variance beyond each tool's own fitted rate. Practically, mean time to failure and maintenance cost differ by only a few percent among calibrated families, but an uncalibrated family's policy can cost up to 20% more. A random forest on raw sensor signals predicts remaining useful life more precisely than any degradation-process model, and a wear-only ablation shows this gap comes mainly from the forest's flexibility, not richer sensor access. The contribution is a validated protocol for testing not just which family fits best, but which of its own structural assumptions are load-bearing.
